# 60-second demo script

*Spoken over the live app, on a phone or a laptop. Timings are cumulative.*

**0:00 – The problem (Advisor page, nothing selected yet)**
"An advisor has ten minutes with a client. Sillage turns that consultation into a three-step routine she can defend, and a follow-up she can send without risk. Everything here runs on about a million real Sephora reviews; the clients are synthetic."

**0:10 – New client (select *Claire D.*)**
"Claire is new, so there is no history to learn from. The app says which path it uses and why: what clients liked most, minus products her dry skin clearly dislikes. Each pick shows its evidence, for example 'liked by 87% of 412 dry-skin reviewers', and figures under 20 reviews are hidden. She avoids fragrance: that's a text filter, and the app says it is not an allergy guarantee."

**0:25 – Returning client (select *Emma R.*)**
"Emma has a history, so a learned model takes over. The model was chosen on three earlier periods and checked once on a held-out period. The reason under each product comes from the model itself, for example how many clients who liked her serum also liked this cream."

**0:35 – Guardrails (scroll to the message, type 'This cream heals your skin, only $5')**
"The draft is built only from the records. If I add a claim or a price that isn't in them, it's blocked and Approve is disabled. Margaux has no marketing consent, so no message is drafted and no AI is called."

**0:45 – The hard case (select *Hannah K.*)**
"Hannah's budget is below the cheapest possible routine. The app says exactly which constraint fails and never relaxes a filter silently."

**0:50 – Results page**
"For managers: [RESULT SENTENCE]. My first model choice failed on the test period, and I kept that in. It's the reason the final protocol uses three validation periods."

**0:60 – Close**
"Next step: a store-level pilot measuring 90-day repeat purchase. The business case has the sample size and the risks."
